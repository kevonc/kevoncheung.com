## Got a dog for the first time

I was afraid of dogs all my life, and I even got bitten by a huge dog once while hiking. But my wife is a huge dog person, she's had multiple dogs in her life. And of course, my two daughters naturally love dogs too, since they play with so many when we're out. I really like how a dog can bring warmth and kindness to a person, so I said "yes" to having a dog in our family. Now I'm really close to Bestie. I take him out on walks and runs myself.

## Speaking a lot more Mandarin

I'm now an education consultant for a company serving Chinese families. I am Chinese and I know Mandarin, but I'd never really spoken it before.

Now I'm interacting with Chinese families and learning a lot about their culture and aspirations. It's really opened my eyes, these families are incredibly ambitious in raising their kids. Growing up in Hong Kong and then the U.S., I feel like we sometimes take things for granted, so it's refreshing to be around people who want to achieve the highest level of success.

## Diversifying income

One truth I think we all learn at a certain age: you can't rely on just one source of income. It'll make your life miserable.

Don't be greedy, diversify, make good decisions. That's my simple guideline for building wealth.

## Girls are doing well

One day I had a chat with my wife about the education path our daughters should have. We asked ourselves, if money weren't a problem, what would we want for them? We both went to boarding school (U.S. and U.K.), and we both agree that independent school life was a huge part of our journey.

Then an interesting follow-up question came up: how should we use our family's resources to create a great path for the girls? I know a lot of people say universities and premium high schools won't matter anymore in the AI world. But is that really true? I'm skeptical.

Right now we spend money carefully and wisely. But one thought I keep coming back to is that we'll probably need to shift to an investment mindset when it comes to our girls' education.

## Busier but happier

When I was running my own business, I had a lot of free time to explore. Now that I work for someone else, I'm busier than ever, there's a lot on my plate and a lot of people to collaborate with. But I'm happier than I've been in years, because the busyness also brings growth. I have bosses who know how to use my skills, so it feels like a fair trade of money and time. And I've been exposed to people, clients, partners, I never would have met otherwise.

## I realized I'm not that introverted

Here's an interesting fact about me: I don't get too close to my closest friends, but I can be surprisingly vulnerable with people I just met. I prefer texting over talking on the phone. For the longest time, I thought I was super introverted, but lately conversations with friends have made me question that. At times I'm the one initiating gatherings and making small talk, if there's a reason behind it. It's starting to feel unclear how to even define introverted vs extroverted. I guess that's what people call ambiverted. Honestly, I think a lot of people are ambiverted these days.

## I'm way too organized

I restart my laptop a few times a day just to reset my mind. I clean up my home now and then, throwing away anything unnecessary. I make plans for everything I do.

## I use AI, and I think it's dumb not to

If I can use AI to make my work easier, I will. I want to save my energy for higher-level thinking. I was intimidated by AI (or maybe just misunderstood it) for the first couple of years, but I've since gotten over that. Now I think it'd be crazy not to use it, the same way it'd be crazy to stick with pen and paper instead of a computer. So why are we still resistant to AI? We should really learn to leverage it.

## I like that I was U.S. educated and influenced

I know there's a lot of noise and criticism about the U.S., but there's no doubt it's the place that injected me with an entrepreneurial spirit. I'd love for my girls to be around people with that same spirit too.
