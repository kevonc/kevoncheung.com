## Life in a glance: my timeline

- **1990:** born
- **2000:** grew up in Hong Kong
- **2003:** tinkered a lot with the Internet and built websites serving thousands of people
- **2005:** at 15, hopped on a plane to the U.S. by myself
- **2008:** did high school at a boarding school called Tabor Academy
- **2012:** did college at Babson College, learning all about entrepreneurship and finance
- **2013:** lived in New York for 3 months picking up coding
- **2014:** got my first job as a software engineer and moved to Singapore within 4 days
- **2015:** ran a kids' coding school across Asia
- **2019:** got married to Lydia
- **2020:** ran a software company and started writing openly online
- **2021:** had a baby and became known for a term called "[Build in Public](https://publiclab.co/build-in-public)"
- **2022:** wrote and published my first book, "[Find Joy in Chaos](https://findjoyinchaos.com/)"
- **2023:** had another baby
- **2024:** started mentoring kids on character, confidence, and spotlight
- **2026:** got a westie dog (first in my life)

## I care about education

I'm an educator who believes AI will forever change how we work, and especially how we learn. When I teach, I always use AI and tech to create learning experiences that actually stick.

I'm an education consultant, and I help students get into top U.S. boarding schools. Having graduated from one myself, I'm a firm believer that it's the best place for a teenager to learn how to thrive, not just for academic excellence, but also for independence, confidence, and curiosity.

I hopped on a plane from Hong Kong to the U.S. at 15, all by myself. I think this is what set me up to be fearless about any path ahead. That's the same fearlessness I want to help my students find. Other than that, I look for the one thing about a kid that admissions can picture on campus within 3 seconds, and help them show up with that character and confidence.

I also have a [manifesto about the future of education here](https://publiclab.co/manifesto).

## I put my family first



![Kevon's Family](/images/kevon-family.jpg)

I love my family.

Growing up, I never imagined myself being family-oriented, until I met my wife. Part of it is probably because I grew up in a single-parent family (my dad got sick and left us early, and I saw how little time he had for us). I wanted to do it differently. Or maybe it's just that I found the right person to walk this path with. Sometimes it takes two to click.

Lydia and I met in 2016. I often joke that it was "wife at first sight." We got married in 2019 and now live a happy life with our two daughters, Avery and Audrey, and one dog, Bestie.

I'm a proud GirlDad. I love watching a kid grow. The messy and unpredictable middle are the best, and that's why I'm drawn to parenting and education work. As for our daughters, I don't have expectations of what I want them to do. I just want them to be driven, passionate, and courageous. I hope that they are willing to try, fail, and pivot.

## How I live

I've held myself to a very high standard in everything I put out, since I was young. I just don't understand mediocre work. I feel good when I put in everything to make something amazing, and people appreciate it.

I'm also disciplined. It's probably because of my personality (I'm an ISTJ), I like systems and routines. I feel like a mess if I don't have these things to guide me in life.

I'm committed. I feel like the majority of the world has less willpower to hold on to things nowadays. It's somewhat sad. I like exploring new things because I'm a Gemini, but for the things I value most in life, I put commitment at the highest priority.

## Life is about connecting the dots

I majored in Entrepreneurship and Finance, but looking back, every step since then has quietly led me toward education and teaching. Fresh out of university, I spent the summer learning how to code and got my first job as a software engineer in Singapore. I hopped on the plane and relocated myself in 4 days. After one year, I realized I didn't want to be typing on the keyboard all day (Yup, before AI showed up, that was normal).

Coincidentally, an entrepreneur reached out and I went on to spend four years expanding a kids coding school across Asia. I never imagined I would be interested in education, but saying yes ended up changing the entire trajectory of my life. After that, I spent two years leading a funded startup building software for online teaching and facilitating. The company was later acquired.

During COVID in 2020, I took a big step into writing and teaching online myself. I wrote a guide, [Build in Public Definitive Guide](https://publiclab.co/build-in-public), that went somewhat viral. With an audience, I went on to publish books and teach entrepreneurs worldwide.

Somewhere along the way, I realized I'd spent years mentoring entrepreneurs to build in public, but the itch to mentor was always there long before that, back when I ran the kids coding school. These days, I've found myself circling back to where it all started, guiding kids again. Having two daughters of my own now, I see firsthand how much good guidance and nurturing shape a kid's growth, and I know there are many more out there who just need someone in their corner.

Turns out, all roads were leading back to kids and learning.
