I know that every family travels differently. Some families plan their trips around sightseeing spots, museums, historical landmarks, etc. 

We ... don't.

We love doing regular, daily things. 

For our family, traveling is centered around learning, especially **hands-on learning**.

<img src="/images/articles/family-travel-cooking.jpg" alt="Our family taking a cooking class in Chiang Mai" class="cover-image" />

At our girls' age (5.5 and 3.5), we don't care as much about whether they are actually learning specific skills. We mostly care about having an immersive experience together rather than just looking at things or listening to information.

That's why taking classes has become a big part of our family travel. Wherever we go, we look for local experiences we can take part in.

When we went to Osaka, we took a mochi-making class with our girls' grandparents! The girls took part in every step, pounding the rice and shaping it into balls. They still remember shouting "yoisho!" as they pounded the mochi.

<img src="/images/articles/family-travel-mochi.jpg" alt="Mochi making with grandparents" />

On the same trip, we also found a restaurant where we could make our own takoyaki at the table. Super easy and fun!

<img src="/images/articles/family-travel-takoyaki.jpg" alt="DIY experience is cool too" />

Then we went to Chiang Mai. 

I'm thankful that Mama Noi Cooking School allowed our girls to join the cooking class because most places don't. Kids can make such a chaotic scene!

But we know our girls. They're well-behaved and eager to participate. So if we're there to guide them, they'll be all right. Of course, we pay close attention to make sure they are safe and don't disrupt the experience.

We took a trip to an elephant sanctuary where we learned about the elephants' living habits and diet.

<img src="/images/articles/family-travel-elephant.jpg" alt="Our family bathing an elephant at a sanctuary in Chiang Mai" />

The Muay Thai class took even more effort. It wasn't something we could simply find and book online. We reached out to Dang Muay Thai and asked if they could arrange a private class for the four of us, including the kids.

Luckily, they said yes! The girls walked into a real Muay Thai gym full of adults training. They immediately said, "This place smells!" That was hilarious, but I was happy they stayed.

<img src="/images/articles/family-travel-muaythai.jpg" alt="Taking a family Muay Thai class in Chiang Mai" />

Overall, the most important thing was that they got involved. Just like with the mochi, the cooking, and the elephants, they were immersed in the experience.

**A lot of family learning experiences aren't obvious until you ask for them.**

It takes extra effort to find places that welcome young kids. Sometimes you have to contact them and create the opportunity yourself. But if hands-on learning is what your family cares about, I think you'd be super surprised by what is possible.
