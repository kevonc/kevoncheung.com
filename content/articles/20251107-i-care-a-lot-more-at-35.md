I’m now sitting alone at Changi Airport in Singapore, ready to head home after my first solo trip in six years.

I came here to immerse myself in the education space. Beyond connecting with amazing educators and old friends, I’ve noticed a profound shift in myself now that I’m 35, a dad of two, and carrying the weight of more life experience than my 20-something self.

I care deeply about the relationships I have.

My dad passed away when I was ten. Other than him and other ancestors, I haven’t made a visit to the columbarium or cemetery for others. This trip, though, I made time to honor an old friend I met in Singapore 11 years ago. I stood there, chatting with his photo for 20 minutes. A few years ago, I couldn’t have pictured myself doing something like this.

I’m more intentional about the connections.

I don’t just show up and catch up with people over a meal anymore.

Before the trip, I thought about a mini thing I could get for each person to show a bit more of my appreciation.

I also meet new and old friends with a real sense of curiosity. I ask a lot more questions and then stay quiet to listen. I’m very curious about how they think.

I genuinely want to stay in touch with them.

I see the connecting dots more.

Back then, if I’d catch the same idea in a movie from yesterday and a conversation with a friend today, I’d file it away as a coincidence and move on.

Now? I smile at the synchronicity across such unrelated moments and wonder about the deeper meaning.

To me, a lot has changed from 22 to 35. I feel pretty good about how I’m showing up for life and the people in it these days. I’m also glad I get this solo time to feel these new feelings properly.
