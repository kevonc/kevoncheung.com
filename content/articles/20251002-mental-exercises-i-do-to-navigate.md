In my last post, I talked about “Why I’m going on a new path”. The truth is, I’ve been navigating this new “kids’ learning” space for two months already.

In my 13-year career so far, I’ve done multiple pivots. When I look back, they’re always somewhat interconnected — software engineering, to kids’ coding school, to SaaS software, to online education. It has always been tech X education.

I’m 35 with two little girls, so pivoting isn’t just about jumping in. It’s about thinking clearly, staying open, and moving with purpose.

So, I’m sharing the mental exercises I lean on to navigate this shift. I want to answer some deep questions to keep myself grounded.

## 1. Confirming My Pivot

What assumptions from my previous space am I carrying over and which might hinder me here?

Pivots can be big or small. I don’t believe in big pivots (meaning jumping from one field to a drastically different one). Life is about accumulation. Life is about investment. It’s much easier to ride on what you already have.

In the last five years (2020-2025), I focused on teaching adults “building in public.” It was a business, marketing, and storytelling strategy. I’ve picked up skills like instructional design, facilitation, program sales, and marketing etc.

So, this pivot from adult to kids’ learning feels manageable. Essentially, most of the experiences are similar. Of course, kids are much more curious and unpredictable. They demand a different approach. But I don’t think it’s hard for me to pick up.

Yes, I can carry over my experience and skills.

But what about my heart? Is there a genuine interest in kids’ learning, or am I just saying it?

I find that too many people lie to themselves about what they want. They make something up and try to convince themselves.

I do the opposite.

I start by soaking up books and sharing my learnings on the internet. This proves two things:

- If I’m happily consuming all this knowledge and it doesn’t feel like I’m working, that’s a jackpot.
- If I’m happily creating, sharing, and interacting with whoever resonates, that’s a jackpot.

So, that’s what I did. I bought 10 books about this topic and dived right in. I started posting on Instagram (@kevon) about parenting and kids’ learning. I am having a lot of fun.

That’s how I confirm my assumption about this pivot.

## 2. Blending Into the Community

Who in this ecosystem aligns with my values, and how can I add value to their work without expecting immediate reciprocity?

Five years ago, I was new to the creator world. What’s an audience? What’s personal branding? Who should I help?

It took me months to get to know the “online community” (99.9% of whom I still haven’t met in real life), so I’m doing the same this time with one twist.

The difference is that I’m not just meeting people online. I’m meeting them offline too.

So far, in the last couple of months, I’ve met so many amazing educators:

- Vincent from Press Start Academy
- Natalie from OWN Academy
- Renee from Talent Co-Op
- Jen from Ying Dutton Education
- Mila from Kigumi Group (here on Substack)
- Mathilde from NewKid.ai (here on Substack)
- Yash from Alpha School

I volunteered at OWN Academy’s Summer Camp (made a recap video). I volunteered at Renee’s new school project. I hopped on Mathilde’s NewKid podcast with my wife, Lydia (her first-ever podcast interview!) And I’ll fly to Singapore soon to join the Design Education Summit with Vincent.

Gosh, the number of creative ideas and clarifying thoughts has been overflowing.

These people are doing incredible work in this space in different ways.

I don’t have any clear ideas about what we’ll do together, but that’s not the point. Over the years, I’ve learned that exchanging notes and offering help is the key to play a part in the ecosystem.

In my book, Find Joy in Chaos, I strongly recommend that people be generous and helpful in the community to build up their presence. I’m walking the walk myself.

Ultimately, it’s about doing great things with the mutual support within the ecosystem.

## 3. Getting Real Experience with Kids

What specific outcomes do I hope to achieve from these experiences, and how will I measure growth—not just for the kids, but for myself?

If you check out my Instagram, you know I love trying stuff with my daughters, but this pivot must go beyond my own parenting.

To become a useful person in this ecosystem, I want to level up on teaching and facilitating kids in an in-person setting. Why? Tech and AI can help kids learn, but only to a certain extent.

So, for my next step, I’ll be looking out for great teaching opportunities where I can design, create, and guide. What topic would be the best fit? I don’t know. That’s to be figured out.

In the case of navigating a new space, success isn’t just about kids learning. It’s me growing too.

## 4. Creating Content to Find My Groove

What does this format or topic reveal about my evolving interests?

I’ve been teaching “building in public” for five years, so the mindset is engraved in my heart.

No matter what I do, I want to share my thoughts and process with the world, no matter how imperfect they are.

I used to do more writing. Lately, I’ve been making short videos. Now I’m resuming my writing again. The desire for creation is like a light - I turn it up to super-bright sometimes or down to a quiet dim sometimes.

One thing content creation does for me is to help me continuously learn about myself. On different days, I’m thinking about AI in education, play-based learning, or parenting hacks.

What should my focus be? I’ve created family content. I’ve written video letters to my daughters (which felt too cheesy after I published them).

What style do I enjoy? I’ve leaned so much on the creation process to give me clarity. With that, I can keep moving forward.

## 5. Building Projects for Fun and Connection

What can I create now that’s genuinely useful for the new community I’m focusing on (parents and kids)?

The last piece is building.

I’m a builder at heart, and I love doing it in public. It keeps my skills sharp and connects me with people.

I built my first project, FunSheets, to turn a keyword into worksheets for kids aged 2-6, mixing Chinese vocab, English sentences, and math. It was more of a fun project to help me get close to other parents and know what’s on their minds.

Now, I’m building my second project, Chatty Bestie, to help kids improve their communication (English) by having an AI bestie to talk to while a parent acts as a mentor.

This changes the learning dynamic.

So far, I’m seeing that my daughter, Avery (4.5yo), really enjoys having me on her side instead of being on the other side of the table, asking her questions and giving her feedback constantly.

For me, too many people only talk about ideas. I’d rather take action and see what sticks. You can’t think your way out; you have to act your way out.

This pivot is a journey, not a race. I can’t rush it.

These mental exercises, through asking myself tough questions, staying open, and building as I go, surely keep me grounded.

If you’re navigating a new space now or later, I hope this helps you.

Cheers.
