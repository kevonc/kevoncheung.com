A few days ago at 4pm, I was waiting for one student to show up at my consulting call. The student, normally very responsive and reliable, still wasn't in the Zoom room by 4:10pm.

I heard nothing from her, so when she finally got there around 4:13pm, I shared an important reminder with her:

> When you know you're going to be late, it only takes a few seconds to send a text letting the other person know, and that you're sorry.
>
> This means the world to the other person.
>
> Many years ago, I worked for one of the biggest names in finance. I met up with him often, and I assumed that because he was so much "more than" me, he wouldn't bother with the extra gesture toward someone like me.
>
> To my surprise, he texted me that he'd be about 10 minutes late, and he was really sorry. That completely changed how I saw him. We don't work together anymore, but to this day, he's still one of the most respected people in my life.

This small gesture shows a lot about you as a person. It means you're considerate, you're respectful, you're reliable.

So seriously, make it a habit.

And of course, never be late to an interview for U.S. boarding schools!
